# Curated vision-board editor: six-phase proposal

Status: assistant proposal, 2026-09-26. The user directed that all six phases take the psychology report and the intended audience into account. This sequence and its feature details are proposals, not claims from the report or final user approvals.

Source: `Vision_Board_Psychology_Behavioral_Design_Research.pdf` (P), especially pp. 1-5. The report says direct vision-board efficacy evidence is sparse; adjacent work motivates hypotheses about self-endorsed goals, process imagery, planning, monitoring, and revision. It does not validate this app or prove that looking at a board causes outcomes.

Intended initial audience: predominantly middle-aged women in Western markets, including liberal women. Recruit and test within that audience across different income, race, ability, relationship, parenting, work, and health contexts. Do not infer politics, values, goals, body preferences, or style from a demographic label. Include people outside the segment in accessibility and exclusion reviews.

## Phase 1 - Calm, reliable canvas

Offer direct selection, move, resize, multi-select, pan, zoom, keyboard access, and dependable save/reopen. Keep the default toolbar small. Put optional grid, snap, spacing, and later other interaction preferences in a compact settings menu. Technical settings serve control and confidence, not as a first-run questionnaire. Grid never appears in image export. **Current state:** the core interaction, grid/snap settings, session undo/redo, and immediate ordered local saves work; durable history and server recovery remain open. **Gate:** representative desktop and touch users can place, move, recover, and export a board without accidental loss or needing instructions; accessible non-canvas item controls remain available.

## Phase 2 - Meaningful pieces, not generic shapes

Make image, phrase, process cue, and optional meaning a coherent board item. Offer personally chosen images, text, and restrained decorative elements; support replace, crop, layers, and thoughtful presets. Ask "What does this stand for to you?" only in context and always allow skip. Keep a text-first path and image attribution. Do not auto-label images as luxury, success, or emotional traits. **Gate:** users can explain why a board item matters, while users who prefer visual play can complete a board without prompt fatigue. [P pp. 1, 3, 5]

## Phase 3 - Hope connected to ordinary life

For one chosen aspiration, optionally add an ordinary-day scene, process image, small action, current obstacle, and user-authored if-then response. Offer reflection-first and visual-first entry, rather than forcing one order. Surface time, energy, money, and relationship tradeoffs only when relevant; avoid a mandatory life audit. **Gate:** users report greater clarity and an achievable next step without increased pressure or shame. Treat any behavioral benefit as a hypothesis requiring study. [P pp. 1-4]

## Phase 4 - Personal style with progressive disclosure

Provide a few well-designed layouts and mood directions, then expose deeper arrangement, typography, palette, borders, frames, and layering controls in context. Keep user photographs and words visually dominant. Use Inter/Prata and the pink product palette for the app shell, not to dictate every board's aesthetic. Support varied ages, cultures, budgets, family forms, and body types in starter media; avoid luxury and body ideals as defaults. **Gate:** users can produce a board that feels personally theirs, not template-owned, with comparable success on mobile and keyboard. [P pp. 3-4]

## Phase 5 - A living, private board

Add gentle user-led revisits, private history, and neutral states: exploring, active, paused, evolving, completed, released. Let users record what changed, reframe meaning, hide old versions, export, and delete. Reminders must be opt-in and easy to quiet. No streaks, rankings, follower counts, or public feed. **Gate:** users can change or release a goal without feeling they failed; check-ins yield useful reflection rather than notification fatigue. [P pp. 2-5]

## Phase 6 - Optional expression and assistance

Offer destination-specific exports, story/reel composition when rights and rendering are ready, and selective sharing with clear privacy boundaries. If AI is added, it should mirror user-authored words, mark inference, show editable suggestions, and ask whether they fit; it must not diagnose, invent hidden motives, or promise outcomes. Collaboration and community are optional and must not turn intimate material into comparison content. **Gate:** users understand what is shared or sent to AI, can reject suggestions easily, and maintain control of board content. [P pp. 3-5]

## Across every phase

- Every prompt is skippable; no forced trauma disclosure or feared-future exercise.
- Private by default; intimate meanings stay out of analytics and session replay.
- Provide keyboard, screen-reader, text-only, and low-bandwidth paths.
- Use inclusive, realistic media and language. Test with the intended audience instead of treating a demographic profile as a persona.
- Measure clarity, autonomy, burden, revisit value, and pressure through interviews and follow-up, not just creation counts or clicks.
- Keep source-report claims, product hypotheses, user decisions, and implemented features distinct.
